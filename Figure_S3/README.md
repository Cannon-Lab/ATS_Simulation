## The balance of ionic currents that sets the value of *V<sub>rest</sub>* is shown by the current – voltage (IV) relation.

The center panel shows a detailed view of all the currents, as a function of membrane potential, in our computational model, for the case of a WT fiber in normal [K+]O = 4.5 mM. The total current (heavy black curve) is the algebraic sum of all the individual currents (dashed lines), and the resting potential (V_rest , black circle) occurs where the total current is balanced to a value of 0 A/cm2 (horizontal line at the midpoint of the graph). The inward rectifier K+ current, I_Kir (red dashed), that determines susceptibility to ATS is one of three outward currents (i.e. > 0) when V= V_rest, with the other two being the Na,K-ATPase pump (I_pump) and the delayed rectifier K current (I_Kdr). Counter-balancing these outward currents are the inward currents conducted by Na+ channels (I_Na) and Cl- channels (I_Cl). The contributions of these inward currents are shaded in the center panel to emphasize that I_Cl (magenta) dominates at negative membrane potentials < -80 mV, whereas I_Na (gray) is dominant and has much larger inward peak for membrane potentials from -80 mV to -20 mV. The impact of varying [K+] is shown by the 4 smaller panels. In low K+ = 2.0 mM (left), the K+ currents shift leftward (E_K more negative) and normally I_Kir easily balances the inward I_Cl (WT, upper left panel). Because the amplitude of I_Cl is relatively small, a severe reduction of I_Kir is required for the balance of currents to fail in the region of -100 mV (scale 0.08, bottom left), causing paradoxical depolarization of V_rest and weakness (bottom left). In high K+ = 8.0 mM, the K+ currents shift rightward, resulting in a mild depolarization of V_rest for WT fibers (upper right). The inward I_Na rapidly becomes large at voltages > -80 mV, and so even a modest reduction of I_Kir (scale = 0.28) shifts the balance of currents for V_rest to be substantially more depolarized until I_Kdr  is activated an resets the balance (bottom right).
