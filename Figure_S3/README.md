## The balance of ionic currents that sets the value of *V<sub>rest</sub>* is shown by the current – voltage (IV) relation.

The center panel shows a detailed view of all the currents, as a function of membrane potential, in our computational model, for the case of a WT fiber in normal [K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>o</sub> = 4.5 mM. The total current (heavy black curve) is the algebraic sum of all the individual currents (dashed lines), and the resting potential (*V<sub>rest</sub>* , black circle) occurs where the total current is balanced to a value of 0 &mu;A/cm<sup>2</sup> (horizontal line at the midpoint of the graph). The inward rectifier K<sup>+</sup> current, *I<sub>Kir</sub>* (red dashed), that determines susceptibility to ATS is one of three outward currents (i.e. > 0) when V= *V<sub>rest</sub>*, with the other two being the Na,K-ATPase pump (*I<sub>pump</sub>*) and the delayed rectifier K current (*I<sub>Kdr</sub>*). Counter-balancing these outward currents are the inward currents conducted by Na<sup>+</sup> channels (*I<sub>Na</sub>*) and Cl- channels (*I<sub>Cl</sub>*). The contributions of these inward currents are shaded in the center panel to emphasize that *I<sub>Cl</sub>* (magenta) dominates at negative membrane potentials < -80 mV, whereas *I<sub>Na</sub>* (gray) is dominant and has much larger inward peak for membrane potentials from -80 mV to -20 mV. The impact of varying [K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>o</sub> is shown by the 4 smaller panels. In low [K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>o</sub> = 2.0 mM (left), the K<sup>+</sup> currents shift leftward (*E<sub>K</sub>* more negative) and normally *I<sub>Kir</sub>* easily balances the inward *I<sub>Cl</sub>* (WT, upper left panel). Because the amplitude of *I<sub>Cl</sub>* is relatively small, a severe reduction of *I<sub>Kir</sub>* is required for the balance of currents to fail in the region of -100 mV (scale 0.08, bottom left), causing paradoxical depolarization of *V<sub>rest</sub>* and weakness (bottom left). In high [K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>o</sub> = 8.0 mM, the K<sup>+</sup> currents shift rightward, resulting in a mild depolarization of *V<sub>rest</sub>* for WT fibers (upper right). The inward *I<sub>Na</sub>* rapidly becomes large at voltages > -80 mV, and so even a modest reduction of *I<sub>Kir</sub>* (scale = 0.28) shifts the balance of currents for *V<sub>rest</sub>* to be substantially more depolarized until *I<sub>Kdr</sub>* is activated an resets the balance (bottom right).
